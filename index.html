Course Overview — the five units, from the blast furnace to the ammonia plant
iThe full course overview for Grade 10 Industrial Chemistry, with the weekly timetable embedded at the top. Unit 1 reviews the four tools you need all year — equations and moles, thermochemistry, kinetics and equilibrium. The role of "The Character" in the History of Science
iI am always struck by the nature of "The Characters" who pioneered science from the 17th to the 20th Century. The great adventurers and discoverers, the possessors of such insight who could make intuitive leaps into the unknown, often taking extreme risks... Was it Nature, or was it Nurture? What sort of people were they? There is a strange place in the English culture for "The Character", often eccentric and fiercely independent, not abiding by society's Norms of the period. They appear throughout English Culture whether literature (e.g. Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes, GK Chesterton's Father Brown, Agatha Christie's characters, John Buchan's Robert Hannay, Orwell, TE Lawrence, Gertrude Bell and the Durrells), in music (e.g. Ruth Gipps, Ethel Smythe, and other rediscovered female composers), and they continue to appear. The History of Science is full of Newtons, Darwins, Roscoes, Faradays, Cavendishes, Perkins... it's a roll-call of Characters of obsessive scientific rigour and immense intellectual firepower, and in no sense "Ordinary". Sometimes lovable, especially in their mistakes, but often not so, as they are often quite 'difficult' characters. What is it that made these amazing few tread quite lonely paths, and what desires and passions drove them?

Please note: This is a work in very early progress!
A reflection on the eccentric, driven personalities — from Newton to Darwin — who shaped the History of Science. (Work in early progress.)

On this archive and AI

I build The GouldianKnot with Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Since that shapes what you are reading, it seems only fair to say how.

Some of what appears here was drafted by Claude — research notes, the author-and-significance write-ups in Gould’s Library, the occasional essay. Some of it is mine. All of it passes through me before it is published: I read it, check it, and correct it rather more often than you might expect. Where a claim rests on a source, the source is named. Where something is inference rather than evidence, the page says so.

I take responsibility for every word here, including the words I did not write myself. I work this way for the pleasure of it, and because it has made the archive more comprehensive and more detailed than I could have managed alone.
