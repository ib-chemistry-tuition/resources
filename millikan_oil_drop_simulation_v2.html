Millikan Oil-Drop Experiment

Determine the elementary charge e the way Millikan & Fletcher's actual 1909–1913 apparatus allowed: a reversible high-voltage field, an ionizing (X-ray) source to re-charge a single drop mid-flight, and thermostat/barometer controls over the air in the chamber.
Spray a droplet, time its free fall with the field OFF, then switch the field ON. If you guessed the wrong plate polarity the drop will fall faster; the right polarity makes it rise or fall more slowly. Time the field-on motion either way — the sign is measured automatically.

Controls added to match the real apparatus

The historical apparatus had more knobs than just a field switch, so this version adds: a reversing polarity switch (Millikan's plates were wired to a battery bank through a reversing key, letting him flip the field without changing its magnitude, which is how the sign of a drop's charge was actually determined); an ionizing X-ray source aimed into the chamber (in the real lab this was an X-ray tube or a small radium sample; firing it knocks electrons on or off a drop already in view, letting the same drop's charge change be measured over and over — this is the experiment Millikan considered his strongest proof that charge is quantized); and a thermostatted, barometer-monitored chamber (temperature and pressure both change air's viscosity via Sutherland's law and the mean free path used in the Cunningham correction, so Millikan controlled both tightly). Voltage, plate separation, and time acceleration remain as before. One knob is intentionally left out: Millikan's actual telescope/eyepiece micrometer for reading droplet position is abstracted here as the two dashed measurement lines.
